[p3] 1.1 Product Definition
[p3]
[p4]                                    PRODUCT DEFINITION
[p4]
[p4] Owómidé MVP is a Collaborative AI Pricing Workspace for mid-to-large businesses
[p4] operating in dynamic, competitive and price-sensitive emerging economies.
[p4]
[p4] It provides a shared environment where businesses and their pricing stakeholders can plan,
[p4] design, create, analyse, review, refine, execute, monitor and manage pricing work across
[p4] products, services, categories, channels, locations and other relevant pricing scopes.
[p4]
[p4] At its core, the workspace functions as a collaborative AI-native pricing builder.
[p4]
[p4] Users bring the products, services or other items they need to price into a common working
[p4] environment; define what they are trying to achieve; construct and document pricing
[p4] strategies, rules, logic and proposals; define the conditions and parameters of the pricing
[p4] approach; reference applicable organisational Guardrails; analyse the financial implications
[p4] of different pricing choices; collaborate with the stakeholders involved; and move the
[p4] resulting pricing work from creation through review, the applicable organisational decision
[p4] path, execution and subsequent monitoring, all with AI embedded throughout the process.
[p4]
[p4] The workspace acts as the operational environment for pricing work rather than merely a
[p4] repository of final prices or an approval system.
[p4]
[p4] It brings together the information, reasoning, workflows, participants and controls involved in
[p4] developing and managing pricing work so that teams no longer have to assemble the
[p4] process across disconnected spreadsheets, ERP exports, messaging applications, email
[p4] threads, documents and meetings.
[p4]
[p4] AI is embedded throughout the workspace as an assisting participant.
[p4]
[p4] Within the permissions and information available to the invoking user, AI can support
[p4] activities such as:
[p4]
[p4]    ● analysing pricing information;
[p4]    ● identifying risks or inconsistencies;
[p4]    ● drafting or refining pricing work;
[p4]    ● summarising inputs and discussions;
[p4]    ● assisting with calculations and scenarios;
[p4]    ● explaining pricing implications;
[p4]    ● retrieving relevant historical context;
[p4]
[p5]     ● supporting strategy development;
[p5]     ● helping users navigate complex pricing tasks.
[p5]
[p5] AI assistance remains attributable and subject to human control.
[p5]
[p5] The workspace supports collaborative pricing work across its lifecycle, including:
[p5]
[p5]     ● organising products, services and other entities being priced;
[p5]     ● viewing and working with relevant pricing and economic information;
[p5]     ● creating and managing pricing initiatives, proposals, strategies, rules and related
[p5]         pricing work;
[p5]     ● defining     pricing   objectives,   assumptions,    parameters    and   conditions   while
[p5]         referencing applicable organisational Guardrails;
[p5]     ● evaluating unit economics and the implications of proposed pricing actions;
[p5]     ● collaborating through shared context, comments, evidence, assignments and
[p5]         contributions;
[p5]     ● maintaining versions of evolving pricing work;
[p5]     ● routing work through appropriate review and organisational decision processes;
[p5]     ● controlling actions through roles, permissions, authority levels and organisational
[p5]         policies;
[p5]     ● executing or recording authorised pricing actions through the mechanisms available
[p5]         in the MVP;
[p5]     ● monitoring the status and progression of active pricing work;
[p5]     ● preserving the rationale, inputs, changes, organisational decision records and
[p5]         outcomes associated with pricing work;
[p5]     ● making historical pricing knowledge searchable and reusable;
[p5]     ● providing AI assistance across relevant parts of the workflow.
[p5]
[p5] The MVP is not simply to digitise pricing approvals. It is to provide businesses and their
[p5] pricing stakeholders with a dedicated collaborative working environment in which
[p5] developing, coordinating and managing pricing work becomes more economically informed,
[p5] efficient, structured, visible, intelligent, reusable and controllable.
[p5]
[p6] 1.2 Product Objective
[p6]
[p7]                                    PRODUCT OBJECTIVE
[p7]
[p7] The objective of the Owómidé MVP is to make the Collaborative AI Pricing Workspace the
[p7] primary working environment in which businesses and their pricing stakeholders
[p7] carry out and coordinate pricing work.
[p7]
[p7] Instead of pricing work being fragmented across spreadsheets, ERP exports, email,
[p7] messaging applications, meetings, documents and individual knowledge, the MVP brings the
[p7] relevant pricing context, analysis, logic, participants, workflows, controls and history into one
[p7] AI-native workspace.
[p7]
[p7] The MVP should enable a pricing team to move a piece of pricing work from an initial need,
[p7] trigger or objective through analysis, construction, collaboration, review, the applicable
[p7] organisational decision path, execution and monitoring without repeatedly reconstructing the
[p7] process across disconnected tools.
[p7]
[p7] The objective is not merely to digitise the existing manual process. The product should
[p7] improve how pricing work itself is performed by making it more structured, context-rich,
[p7] collaborative, intelligent, traceable and reusable.
[p7]
[p7] The objective is to enable a pricing team to collaboratively turn a pricing need,
[p7] objective or market/business trigger into a well-informed, controlled and executable
[p7] pricing action, while preserving the context, logic, collaboration and history behind
[p7] that work.
[p7]
[p7] A pricing need may begin through:
[p7]
[p7]    ● a supplier or product cost change;
[p7]    ● margin deterioration;
[p7]    ● a competitor price movement;
[p7]    ● a promotion or campaign;
[p7]    ● a scheduled pricing review;
[p7]    ● a new product or service launch;
[p7]    ● inventory conditions requiring markdown or repricing;
[p7]    ● a commercial opportunity;
[p7]    ● a stakeholder request;
[p7]    ● creation or revision of a pricing strategy or rule;
[p7]    ● an external market or business condition change;
[p7]    ● a management objective, mandate or guardrail.
[p7]
[p8] The core pricing lifecycle is:
[p8]
[p8] Understand context → Frame objective → Develop pricing approach → Analyse
[p8] implications → Collaborate → Apply organisational controls → Decide → Execute →
[p8] Monitor → Preserve and reuse knowledge
[p8]
[p8] The MVP should enable teams to:
[p8]
[p8]    1. Understand the pricing context
[p8]        Bring together the product, current price, cost information, commercial information,
[p8]        previous decisions, supporting evidence and other available inputs required to
[p8]        understand the pricing situation.
[p8]    2. Frame the pricing objective
[p8]        Establish what the pricing work is intended to accomplish, what it affects, the
[p8]        assumptions involved and the constraints within which the team must operate.
[p8]    3. Develop the pricing approach
[p8]        Create and refine the proposed pricing action, strategy, rule or logic, including
[p8]        relevant parameters, conditions and calculations while referencing applicable
[p8]        organisational Guardrails.
[p8]    4. Analyse the implications
[p8]        Evaluate unit economics, financial consequences, risks, trade-offs, alternatives and
[p8]        other available evidence.
[p8]    5. Collaborate around the work
[p8]        Allow relevant stakeholders to contribute information, evidence, comments, revisions
[p8]        and expertise while working from the same pricing context.
[p8]    6. Apply organisational control
[p8]        Ensure pricing work respects roles, authority levels, applicable Guardrails, item-level
[p8]        Approval or Policy Authorisation requirements, and organisational policies.
[p8]    7. Reach and execute a decision
[p8]        Progress the work through required review and the applicable organisational decision
[p8]        path, then translate valid execution authority into an executable or recorded pricing
[p8]        action.
[p8]    8. Preserve the decision context
[p8]        Retain the reasoning, information, versions, participants, organisational decision
[p8]        records, changes and actions associated with the pricing decision.
[p8]
[p9]    9. Monitor the work after decision
[p9]        Maintain visibility into implementation, status and relevant subsequent outcomes
[p9]        rather than treating the organisational decision as the end of the process.
[p9]    10.Reuse organisational pricing knowledge
[p9]        Make previous pricing work, rationale, decisions, strategies and patterns available as
[p9]        context for future pricing activity.
[p9]
[p9]
[p9] Role of AI
[p9]
[p9] Owómidé is AI-native rather than a conventional workflow product with AI added as a
[p9] secondary feature.
[p9]
[p9] AI should operate across the pricing workflow rather than exist only as a standalone chatbot.
[p9]
[p9] Where appropriate and permitted, AI should help users:
[p9]
[p9]    ● understand pricing information;
[p9]    ● construct pricing work;
[p9]    ● analyse implications;
[p9]    ● surface relevant context;
[p9]    ● identify inconsistencies and risks;
[p9]    ● synthesise stakeholder input;
[p9]    ● explore alternatives;
[p9]    ● explain calculations and decisions;
[p9]    ● retrieve organisational pricing knowledge;
[p9]    ● reduce repetitive analytical and coordination work.
[p9]
[p9] Human users retain governance control over consequential pricing work in the MVP.
[p9]
[p9] AI assists the pricing process but does not independently acquire Approval or Policy
[p9] Authorisation authority, create execution authority, circumvent organisational controls or
[p9] perform actions outside the permissions and context available to the invoking user.
[p9]
[p9] Where Policy Authorisation is enabled, authority derives from the organisation's configured
[p9] non-AI policy mechanism.
[p9]
[p9]
[p9] Product Success at the Job Level
[p9]
[p9] The MVP succeeds at its core job when a real pricing team can use Owómidé as the primary
[p9] environment for completing actual pricing work from initiation to execution and monitoring,
[p9]
[p10] with substantially less dependence on disconnected spreadsheets, messages, email chains
[p10] and undocumented institutional knowledge.
[p10]
[p10] The critical test is not whether users can simply create or approve a price.
[p10]
[p10] It is whether a meaningful unit of real pricing work can live, progress and remain
[p10] intelligible inside Owómidé from beginning to end.
[p10]
[p11] 1.3 Target Customer
[p11]
[p12]                                         TARGET CUSTOMER
[p12]
[p12] The Owómidé MVP is built for mid-to-large businesses operating in emerging
[p12] economies whose pricing activities are sufficiently complex, frequent, distributed or
[p12] commercially important that manual and semi-automated pricing processes create material
[p12] operational, financial or competitive problems.
[p12]
[p12] The customer is the organisation.
[p12]
[p12] Pricing teams and pricing-adjacent functions are the primary users through whom the
[p12] organisation receives value.
[p12]
[p12] Owómidé is particularly relevant where pricing involves multiple products, services,
[p12] locations, channels, stakeholder groups, pricing rules or recurring pricing decisions, and
[p12] where the business currently coordinates this work through combinations of spreadsheets,
[p12] ERP systems, email, messaging applications, meetings and other disconnected tools.
[p12]
[p12]
[p12] Initial Segment Priority
[p12]
[p12] The MVP should remain horizontally capable of supporting pricing work across industries
[p12] while product development and go-to-market activity initially concentrate on segments for
[p12] which current research provides the strongest evidence.
[p12]
[p12] Primary initial customer segments
[p12]
[p12]       ● Multi-location retail chains
[p12]       ● Multi-vendor marketplaces
[p12]       ● High-catalogue commerce and e-commerce businesses
[p12]       ● Mid-to-large manufacturing businesses managing product portfolios
[p12]
[p12] For     commerce-oriented      organisations,   strong-fit   characteristics   include   managing
[p12] approximately 500 or more SKUs and operating across two or more sales channels.
[p12]
[p12] Secondary initial customer segments
[p12]
[p12]       ● Mid-to-large accommodation and hospitality businesses managing rates across
[p12]          locations or distribution channels
[p12]       ● Mid-to-large transportation and mobility businesses managing dynamic fares or rates
[p12]          across routes, locations or channels
[p12]
[p13] These segments guide initial product validation and commercial focus. They do not
[p13] permanently restrict Owómidé to these industries.
[p13]
[p13]

[p13] work.
[p13]

[p13]

[p13]    ● Category managers
[p13]    ● Revenue managers
[p13]    ● Commercial pricing specialists
[p13]    ● Other employees whose primary responsibility includes analysing, constructing or
[p13]         managing prices
[p13]
[p13] Indirect pricing users
[p13]
[p13]    ● Finance
[p13]    ● Commercial leadership
[p13]    ● Sales
[p13]    ● Procurement
[p13]    ● Operations
[p13]    ● Category and brand leadership
[p13]    ● Executives
[p13]    ● Compliance or audit
[p13]    ● Marketplace or vendor management
[p13]    ● Pricing operations and implementation personnel
[p13]
[p13] The MVP must therefore support pricing as a cross-functional organisational workflow,
[p13] rather than assume that pricing is performed by a single specialist or department.
[p13]
[p13]

[p13]
[p13] Strong-fit customer environments commonly involve:
[p13]
[p13]    ● manual or semi-automated pricing processes;
[p13]
[p14]    ● several systems or communication channels involved in pricing work;
[p14]    ● relevant pricing information distributed across people and tools;
[p14]    ● multiple stakeholders contributing to or controlling pricing decisions;
[p14]    ● pricing activity across multiple products, categories, locations or channels;

[p16] 1.4 Problem Definition
[p16]
[p17]                                    PROBLEM DEFINITION
[p17]
[p17] “In emerging economies, mid-to-large businesses lose 10–20% of margins, miss
[p17] 30–38% of revenue opportunities, and waste 15+ hours weekly because fragmented,
[p17] manual workflows lack real-time market visibility in environments where nearly 90%
[p17] of commerce occurs offline and is fast-moving.”
[p17]
[p17] The problem is not simply that businesses use spreadsheets, email or manual approval
[p17] processes.
[p17]
[p17] The deeper issue is that pricing is a continuous, distributed and cross-functional business
[p17] process, while most organisations still manage it as a collection of disconnected activities.
[p17]
[p17] A single pricing action can depend on things like:
[p17]
[p17]    ● cost information;
[p17]    ● margins;
[p17]    ● competitor prices;
[p17]    ● demand;
[p17]    ● inventory;
[p17]    ● supplier conditions;
[p17]    ● customer behaviour;
[p17]    ● commercial objectives;
[p17]    ● regulatory constraints;
[p17]    ● historical decisions;
[p17]    ● management judgement.
[p17]
[p17] These inputs are often owned by different teams and stored across different systems.
[p17]
[p17] The organisation must repeatedly bring these pieces together before it can make, approve
[p17] and execute a pricing decision.
[p17]
[p17]
[p17] 1. Fragmented Pricing Workflows
[p17]
[p17] Pricing work is commonly distributed across ERP systems, spreadsheets, BI dashboards,
[p17] email, messaging applications, meetings, internal tools and individual working methods.
[p17]
[p17] Each tool may perform its own function, but the pricing work itself does not necessarily exist
[p17] as one continuous process.
[p17]
[p18] This forces teams to manually move information and decisions between systems and
[p18] stakeholders throughout the pricing lifecycle.
[p18]
[p18]
[p18] 2. Pricing Depends on Distributed Organisational Knowledge
[p18]
[p18] Pricing is rarely controlled by one person or department.
[p18]
[p18] Different functions contribute different parts of the decision:
[p18]
[p18]    ● Finance contributes cost, margin and financial implications.
[p18]    ● Sales contributes customer behaviour, demand and commercial feedback.
[p18]    ● Pricing, revenue or category teams structure and analyse pricing work.
[p18]    ● Procurement contributes supplier and input-cost information.
[p18]    ● Marketing contributes market, promotion and demand context.
[p18]    ● Operations contributes inventory and execution realities.
[p18]    ● Commercial leadership determines strategic direction.
[p18]    ● Management provides authority for consequential decisions.
[p18]    ● Legal or compliance functions may impose regulatory constraints.
[p18]    ● Operational teams and systems execute the resulting pricing action.
[p18]
[p18] The challenge is not simply getting people to collaborate.
[p18]
[p18] The organisation must coordinate specialised responsibilities while maintaining one coherent
[p18] pricing context.
[p18]
[p18]
[p18] 3. Pricing Teams Spend Too Much Time Assembling the Decision
[p18]
[p18] A significant part of pricing work is spent gathering, checking, reconciling and interpreting
[p18] information before the actual decision can be made.
[p18]
[p18] Users may need to obtain:
[p18]
[p18]    ● cost information from finance;
[p18]    ● sales performance from ERP systems;
[p18]    ● competitor information from market visits;
[p18]    ● supplier updates from procurement;
[p18]    ● customer feedback from sales;
[p18]    ● historical information from previous spreadsheets or reports.
[p18]
[p19] Even when the information exists somewhere in the organisation, assembling it into usable
[p19] pricing context remains largely manual.
[p19]
[p19] This creates repeated analytical and coordination work every time pricing work is initiated.
[p19]
[p19]
[p19] 4. Market Conditions Move Faster Than Pricing Processes
[p19]
[p19] Pricing decisions in emerging economies can be exposed to fast-changing conditions
[p19] including:
[p19]
[p19]    ● FX movements;
[p19]    ● inflation;
[p19]    ● input and supplier costs;
[p19]    ● logistics costs;
[p19]    ● competitor pricing;
[p19]    ● customer purchasing power;
[p19]    ● demand changes;
[p19]    ● inventory conditions;
[p19]    ● promotions;
[p19]    ● government policy;
[p19]    ● taxation;
[p19]    ● regulation;
[p19]    ● seasonal behaviour;
[p19]    ● distribution conditions;
[p19]    ● market availability.
[p19]
[p19] These conditions can change continuously while internal pricing processes may still require
[p19] multiple handoffs, reviews, meetings and approvals.
[p19]
[p19] This creates a mismatch between the speed of the market and the speed of the
[p19] organisation's pricing response.
[p19]
[p19]
[p19] 5. Offline Market Structure Creates an Information Gap
[p19]
[p19] A significant proportion of commercial activity in emerging economies occurs outside fully
[p19] digitised channels.
[p19]
[p19] Competitor prices, distributor behaviour, open-market prices, supplier movements and other
[p19] commercially relevant signals may require:
[p19]
[p20]    ● manual field research;
[p20]    ● store visits;
[p20]    ● phone calls;
[p20]    ● supplier relationships;
[p20]    ● informal market networks.
[p20]
[p20] Businesses may therefore have strong internal ERP and BI capabilities while still lacking
[p20] timely visibility into what is happening outside their own systems.
[p20]
[p20] Pricing decisions can consequently depend on information that is incomplete or already stale
[p20] by the time it reaches the decision-maker.
[p20]
[p20]
[p20] 6. Pricing Analysis Requires Continuous Synthesis
[p20]
[p20] The difficult part of pricing is often not calculating a percentage or changing a price field.
[p20]
[p20] The harder task is determining how multiple variables should influence the decision at the
[p20] same time.
[p20]
[p20] Businesses may need to balance:
[p20]
[p20]    ● profitability against competitiveness;
[p20]    ● margin against volume;
[p20]    ● market share against short-term profit;
[p20]    ● customer affordability against cost inflation;
[p20]    ● stock clearance against margin protection;
[p20]    ● growth targets against market conditions;
[p20]    ● commercial urgency against governance requirements.
[p20]
[p20] Pricing therefore requires continuous synthesis of financial, market, operational and strategic
[p20] information.
[p20]
[p20] Where this synthesis remains manual, the quality and speed of decisions become heavily
[p20] dependent on individual experience.
[p20]
[p20]
[p20] 7. Cross-Functional Coordination Creates Decision Latency
[p20]
[p20] Pricing work frequently moves between several people before execution.
[p20]
[p21] One stakeholder performs analysis. Another challenges the assumptions. Finance
[p21] recalculates the economics. Sales provides market feedback. Commercial leadership
[p21] reviews the recommendation. Management provides approval. Another team implements the
[p21] resulting decision.
[p21]
[p21] Changes at one stage can force earlier stages to be repeated.
[p21]
[p21] This creates back-and-forth coordination, duplicated work, follow-ups and waiting time that
[p21] extend the pricing cycle.
[p21]
[p21]
[p21] 8. Decision Context Is Easily Lost Between Stages
[p21]
[p21] As pricing work moves between people and systems, organisations can lose continuity
[p21] around:
[p21]
[p21]    ● why the pricing work started;
[p21]    ● which information was used;
[p21]    ● which assumptions were made;
[p21]    ● what alternatives were considered;
[p21]    ● what changed during review;
[p21]    ● which version is current;
[p21]    ● who contributed to the decision;
[p21]    ● what was approved;
[p21]    ● what was ultimately implemented.
[p21]
[p21] The resulting price may remain in an ERP while the reasoning that produced it remains
[p21] distributed across spreadsheets, messages, emails and individual knowledge.
[p21]
[p21]
[p21] 9. Pricing Errors Become Margin and Revenue Problems
[p21]
[p21] When costs change without prices being reviewed quickly enough, businesses can continue
[p21] operating with economically incorrect prices.
[p21]
[p21] When market conditions move faster than the organisation responds, the business can
[p21] remain overpriced, underpriced or commercially misaligned.
[p21]
[p21] When pricing analysis does not contain the full cost or commercial context, a decision that
[p21] initially appears profitable can become loss-making once logistics, marketing, supplier or
[p21] other costs are considered.
[p21]
[p22] These failures connect operational pricing weakness directly to margin leakage and missed
[p22] revenue opportunities.
[p22]
[p22]
[p22] 10. Organisations Lack a Continuous Pricing Operating Environment
[p22]
[p22] Most businesses already have tools for individual parts of the pricing process.
[p22]
[p22] The missing layer is the environment connecting the work together.
[p22]
[p22] The organisation needs a place where pricing work can retain its:
[p22]
[p22]    ● context;
[p22]    ● data;
[p22]    ● analysis;
[p22]    ● assumptions;
[p22]    ● strategy;
[p22]    ● contributors;
[p22]    ● workflow;
[p22]    ● decisions;
[p22]    ● approvals;
[p22]    ● execution state;
[p22]    ● history.
[p22]
[p22] Without that continuity, every pricing initiative becomes another coordination exercise.
[p22]
[p22]
[p22] MVP Problem Scope
[p22]
[p22] The MVP focuses on reducing the fragmentation, manual coordination and decision friction
[p22] involved in how mid-to-large businesses perform pricing work by providing a continuous
[p22] AI-native workspace for planning, analysing, constructing, collaborating on, governing,
[p22] executing, monitoring and preserving pricing work.
[p22]
[p23] 1.5 Product Outcome
[p23]
[p24]                                    PRODUCT OUTCOME
[p24]
[p24] The MVP should be judged by whether it materially improves how businesses perform
[p24] pricing work, not by whether individual features are used.
[p24]
[p24] Success means Owómidé becomes a working environment through which real pricing
[p24] activity can be completed more efficiently, intelligently and controllably than through the
[p24] organisation's existing process.
[p24]
[p24]
[p24] 1. Business Outcomes
[p24]
[p24] The MVP should help businesses:
[p24]
[p24]    ● reduce avoidable margin leakage caused by delayed, incorrect or poorly informed
[p24]        pricing actions;
[p24]    ● capture more revenue opportunities by responding faster to pricing needs and
[p24]        changing commercial conditions;
[p24]    ● reduce the employee time consumed by manual pricing analysis, coordination,
[p24]        follow-ups and administrative work;
[p24]    ● improve visibility and control over pricing activity across the organisation;
[p24]    ● reduce pricing errors and economically unsafe decisions;
[p24]    ● improve the consistency and quality of pricing decisions.
[p24]
[p24] The broader validated business problem establishes margin loss, missed revenue
[p24] opportunities and 15+ hours of weekly pricing-related waste as the economic context against
[p24] which these outcomes should ultimately be evaluated.
[p24]
[p24]
[p24] 2. Pricing Workflow Outcomes
[p24]
[p24] The MVP should enable a meaningful unit of pricing work to progress through Owómidé from
[p24] initiation to completion without the core workflow depending on disconnected spreadsheets,
[p24] email chains or messaging threads.
[p24]
[p24] A successful workflow should allow users to:
[p24]
[p24]    ● establish the pricing objective and scope;
[p24]    ● bring together the relevant pricing context;
[p24]    ● develop and analyse the pricing approach;
[p24]    ● collaborate with required stakeholders;
[p24]
[p25]    ● review and revise the work;
[p25]    ● apply relevant guardrails and organisational controls;
[p25]    ● complete the applicable organisational decision path and establish valid execution
[p25]        authority;
[p25]    ● record or complete execution;
[p25]    ● monitor the resulting state;
[p25]    ● retain the complete history and rationale.
[p25]
[p25] The intended outcome is a measurable reduction in pricing cycle time, coordination effort
[p25] and unnecessary back-and-forth.
[p25]
[p25]
[p25] 3. Decision Quality Outcomes
[p25]
[p25] The MVP should improve the quality of pricing decisions by ensuring that users can evaluate
[p25] relevant financial, commercial and operational implications before execution.
[p25]
[p25] Users should be able to understand:
[p25]
[p25]    ● the current pricing position;
[p25]    ● the proposed pricing action;
[p25]    ● the economic impact of that action;
[p25]    ● relevant assumptions and constraints;
[p25]    ● important risks or guardrail breaches;
[p25]    ● alternative scenarios where applicable;
[p25]    ● the reasoning supporting the final decision.
[p25]
[p25] The product should reduce the likelihood that consequential pricing actions are taken without
[p25] sufficient context or economic understanding.
[p25]
[p25]
[p25] 4. Collaboration and Organisational Alignment Outcomes
[p25]
[p25] The MVP should create one shared pricing context for the people involved in a pricing
[p25] workflow.
[p25]
[p25] Success means stakeholders can understand:
[p25]
[p25]    ● what pricing work is active;
[p25]    ● what stage it is currently in;
[p25]    ● who is responsible for each required action;
[p25]    ● what information and assumptions are being used;
[p25]
[p26]    ● what has changed;
[p26]    ● what input is required from them;
[p26]    ● what has already been reviewed or decided, including Approval or Policy
[p26]        Authorisation where applicable;
[p26]    ● what happens next.
[p26]
[p26] The product should reduce dependence on manual status updates, repeated explanations
[p26] and stakeholder chasing.
[p26]

[p26] 5. Governance and Control Outcomes
[p26]
[p26] Pricing work should remain governed according to the organisation's defined roles,
[p26] permissions, authority, Guardrails and applicable organisational decision requirements.
[p26]
[p26] Success means:
[p26]
[p26]    ● users can perform only actions they are authorised to perform;
[p26]    ● applicable Approval or Policy Authorisation requirements cannot be bypassed;
[p26]    ● Approval or Policy Authorisation authority remains bound to the exact controlled
[p26]        Pricing Proposal version against which it was established;
[p26]    ● material changes must not inherit Approval or Policy Authorisation authority from an
[p26]        earlier Pricing Proposal version;
[p26]    ● pricing guardrails are applied consistently;
[p26]    ● sensitive pricing and cost information respects access restrictions;
[p26]    ● consequential actions remain attributable to specific users;
[p26]    ● the history of the pricing workflow remains auditable.
[p26]
[p26] Governance should provide control without unnecessarily slowing down routine pricing work.

[p26] 6. AI Outcomes

[p26]    ● analyse pricing information;
[p26]    ● construct or refine pricing work;
[p26]    ● identify risks and inconsistencies;
[p26]
[p27]    ● evaluate scenarios and alternatives;

[p27]    ● retrieve relevant historical information;

[p27] AI output must remain explainable within the context of the user's task, constrained by the

[p27] 8. MVP Activation Definition
[p27]

[p27] including analysis, collaboration, required review, completion of the applicable organisational
[p27] decision path, and a completed or recorded pricing action.

[p29] 10. MVP Success Definition
[p29]
[p29] The MVP is successful when target businesses repeatedly use Owómidé to complete real
[p29] pricing work and demonstrate that the workspace produces a materially better pricing
[p29] process than their previous operating method.
[p29]
[p29] At minimum, success requires evidence that Owómidé:
[p29]
[p29]    1. becomes part of the organisation's recurring pricing workflow;
[p29]    2. reduces manual coordination and fragmented pricing work;
[p29]    3. shortens the time required to move pricing work from initiation to execution;
[p29]    4. improves visibility into pricing context, responsibilities and decision state;
[p29]    5. provides useful AI assistance during real pricing work;
[p29]    6. preserves reliable pricing history and accountability;
[p29]    7. improves organisational control over pricing decisions; and
[p29]    8. produces measurable operational or economic value sufficient for customers to
[p29]        continue paying for the product.
[p29]
[p29] Feature completion alone does not constitute MVP success. The MVP succeeds only when
[p29] customers repeatedly use it for consequential pricing work and prefer that operating model
[p29] to the process it replaces.

[p32] 2.1 Product Model
[p32]
[p33]                                      PRODUCT MODEL
[p33]
[p33] The Owómidé MVP should organise pricing work around a small set of connected product
[p33] objects rather than treating individual screens, spreadsheets or approval requests as
[p33] isolated activities.
[p33]
[p33]
[p33]  1.    Organisation Workspace
[p33]
[p33]
[p33] The Organisation Workspace is the top-level environment for a customer organisation.
[p33]
[p33] It contains the organisation's:
[p33]
[p33]     ● users and teams;
[p33]     ● products, services and other priceable entities;
[p33]     ● pricing initiatives;
[p33]     ● organisational pricing policies;
[p33]     ● roles and permissions;
[p33]     ● pricing guardrails;
[p33]     ● workflow configurations;
[p33]     ● pricing history and organisational knowledge.
[p33]
[p33] Users operate inside the same organisational environment while seeing and performing only
[p33] the actions permitted by their assigned authority and scope.
[p33]
[p33]
[p33]  2.    Pricing Initiative
[p33]
[p33]
[p33] The Pricing Initiative is the primary unit of pricing work in the MVP.
[p33]
[p33] It represents a defined piece of pricing work being undertaken for a specific business
[p33] purpose.
[p33]
[p33] Examples include:
[p33]
[p33]     ● repricing products following a cost increase;
[p33]     ● reviewing a category's pricing;
[p33]     ● launching a new product;
[p33]     ● designing a promotion;
[p33]     ● clearing ageing inventory;
[p33]     ● responding to a competitor movement;
[p33]
[p34]    ● revising a pricing strategy;
[p34]    ● changing prices for a customer, region or channel;
[p34]    ● performing a scheduled price review.
[p34]
[p34] A Pricing Initiative should contain the complete context required to understand and progress
[p34] that work from initiation through completion.
[p34]
[p34] It can include:
[p34]
[p34]    ● objective and rationale;
[p34]    ● affected pricing scope;
[p34]    ● structured Pricing Context and supporting Evidence;
[p34]    ● assumptions;
[p34]    ● pricing strategy or logic;
[p34]    ● calculations and analysis;
[p34]    ● scenarios;
[p34]    ● stakeholder contributions;
[p34]    ● proposals;
[p34]    ● versions;
[p34]    ● organisational decision records, including Approval and Policy Authorisation records;
[p34]    ● execution records;
[p34]    ● monitoring information;
[p34]    ● complete activity history.
[p34]
[p34] This becomes the persistent container that prevents pricing context from being lost as work
[p34] moves between people and stages.
[p34]
[p34]
[p34]  3.   Pricing Scope
[p34]
[p34]
[p34] Every Pricing Initiative must define what the pricing work applies to.
[p34]
[p34] Pricing Scope represents the Priceable Entities and pricing context to which the pricing work
[p34] applies.
[p34]
[p34] A Pricing Scope must contain one or more Priceable Entities defined in DATA-001.
[p34]
[p34] Priceable Entities may be included in scope through:
[p34]
[p34]    ● direct selection of individual Priceable Entities;
[p34]
[p35]    ● selection of a supported organisational grouping, such as a category or product
[p35]        group, that resolves to multiple Priceable Entities;
[p35]    ● another supported scope-selection mechanism that resolves to identifiable Priceable
[p35]        Entities.
[p35]
[p35] The Pricing Scope may additionally be qualified by applicable pricing dimensions such as:
[p35]
[p35]    ● location;
[p35]    ● region;
[p35]    ● channel;
[p35]    ● customer group;
[p35]    ● business unit;
[p35]    ● vendor;
[p35]    ● price list;
[p35]    ● other organisation-defined pricing dimensions.
[p35]
[p35] Categories, product groups and pricing dimensions may therefore determine which Priceable
[p35] Entities or pricing contexts are included in scope, but they must not be treated as Priceable
[p35] Entities solely because they are used for scope selection.
[p35]
[p35] Where a grouping selector is used, the system must be able to determine which Priceable
[p35] Entities are included by that selector.
[p35]
[p35] The same Pricing Initiative may apply to multiple Priceable Entities and multiple
[p35] pricing-dimension combinations where the pricing action is logically managed as one piece
[p35] of work.
[p35]
[p35] At any consequential decision point, the applicable Pricing Scope must be sufficiently
[p35] resolved to determine the exact Priceable Entities and pricing dimensions affected by the
[p35] proposed action.
[p35]
[p35]
[p35]  4.   Pricing Strategy and Logic
[p35]
[p35]
[p35] The workspace must allow users to express how the pricing decision should work.
[p35]
[p35] This includes:
[p35]
[p35]    ● pricing objective;
[p35]    ● strategy;
[p35]    ● calculation logic;
[p35]
[p36]    ● rules;
[p36]    ● conditions;
[p36]    ● parameters;
[p36]    ● assumptions;
[p36]    ● thresholds;
[p36]    ● constraints;
[p36]    ● references to applicable organisational guardrails.
[p36]
[p36] Organisational Guardrails remain Governance-owned controls. Pricing Strategy and Logic
[p36] may reference applicable Guardrails but must not independently create, redefine or own
[p36] them.
[p36]
[p36] The MVP should represent this logic in a human-readable format so authorised stakeholders
[p36] can understand, review and challenge it without requiring technical expertise.
[p36]
[p36] For example:
[p36]
[p36] Increase selling price by 8% across the selected products while maintaining gross
[p36] margin above 15%.
[p36]
[p36] More complex pricing logic can combine multiple conditions and rules where required.
[p36]
[p36]
[p36]  5.    Pricing Analysis and Scenarios
[p36]
[p36]
[p36] Pricing Initiatives should support analysis of the economic and commercial implications of
[p36] proposed actions.
[p36]
[p36] Users should be able to evaluate relevant information such as:
[p36]
[p36]    ● Current Price or another explicitly identified pricing baseline;
[p36]    ● proposed price;
[p36]    ● Applicable Unit Cost and relevant cost basis, where required;
[p36]    ● gross margin;
[p36]    ● markup;
[p36]    ● gross profit;
[p36]    ● price change percentage;
[p36]    ● alternative pricing scenarios;
[p36]    ● expected or manually entered business implications.
[p36]
[p37] A Scenario represents an alternative pricing approach that can be evaluated before the
[p37] team commits to a proposal.
[p37]
[p37] Scenarios remain exploratory until explicitly selected or incorporated into the active pricing
[p37] work.
[p37]
[p37]
[p37]  6.     Pricing Proposal
[p37]
[p37]
[p37] A Pricing Proposal is a specific proposed action produced from the work inside a Pricing
[p37] Initiative.
[p37]
[p37] It represents what the team is proposing to do, not the entire pricing initiative itself.
[p37]
[p37] A proposal should contain the exact:
[p37]
[p37]     ● affected scope;
[p37]     ● Current Price or other explicitly identified pricing baseline used;
[p37]     ● proposed pricing state;
[p37]     ● supporting rationale;
[p37]     ● assumptions;
[p37]     ● relevant analysis;
[p37]     ● applicable Guardrail evaluation and configured behavioural treatment;
[p37]     ● applicable organisational decision requirements.
[p37]
[p37] Once a Draft Pricing Proposal is formally submitted into the applicable organisational
[p37] decision path, that same Proposal version is frozen as the controlled decision snapshot.
[p37]
[p37] Submission does not create a new Proposal version solely because the Proposal entered
[p37] the decision path.
[p37]
[p37] A material change after submission must create a new controlled Proposal version rather
[p37] than silently modifying the version previously submitted for approval or policy authorisation.
[p37]
[p37]
[p37]  7.     Workflow
[p37]
[p37]
[p37] Each Pricing Initiative progresses through the canonical Pricing Initiative lifecycle defined in
[p37] Section 2.2.
[p37]
[p37] The canonical lifecycle is:
[p37]
[p38] Initiated → In Development → In Review → Awaiting Approval → Ready for Execution →
[p38] Implemented → Monitoring → Completed
[p38]
[p38] Workflow governs how pricing work progresses through the applicable review, organisational
[p38] decision and execution-readiness requirements within that canonical lifecycle.
[p38]
[p38] Workflow may determine or coordinate:
[p38]
[p38]     ● required reviewer actions within the canonical In Review stage;
[p38]     ● responsible participants;
[p38]     ● review completion requirements;
[p38]     ● the applicable organisational decision path;
[p38]     ● item-level approval where applicable;
[p38]     ● policy-authorisation routing where applicable;
[p38]     ● approval sequence and routing;
[p38]     ● Changes Requested and revision loops;
[p38]     ● bounded escalation;
[p38]     ● authorised delegation where supported;
[p38]     ● execution-readiness gating;
[p38]     ● attributable workflow history.
[p38]
[p38] Organisational     differences   must   be   represented   through    the   bounded    Workflow
[p38] Configuration Profile model defined in Section 2.4 and Section 3.8.
[p38]
[p38] Workflow configuration must not create alternative Pricing Initiative lifecycle states, arbitrary
[p38] workflow graphs or unrestricted user-defined workflow automation.
[p38]
[p38] Pricing Proposal decision states and approval progress must remain separate from Pricing
[p38] Initiative lifecycle state.
[p38]
[p38]
[p38]  8.    Approval Record
[p38]
[p38]
[p38] An Approval Record captures an authorised user's formal decision on a specific proposal
[p38] version.
[p38]
[p38] It should preserve:
[p38]
[p38]     ● approver;
[p38]     ● decision;
[p38]     ● timestamp;
[p38]
[p39]    ● proposal version;
[p39]    ● comments or conditions;
[p39]    ● authority under which the action was taken.
[p39]
[p39] Approvals must attach to the exact version reviewed.
[p39]
[p39] A material change after Approval must create a new controlled Pricing Proposal version and
[p39] complete the applicable organisational decision requirements before new execution authority
[p39] can be established. The earlier Approval must remain bound only to the Proposal version
[p39] against which it was made.
[p39]
[p39]
[p39]  9.   Policy Authorisation Record
[p39]
[p39]
[p39] A Policy Authorisation Record captures execution authority established for an exact
[p39] controlled Pricing Proposal version through the organisation's configured policy-authorisation
[p39] mechanism rather than through item-level human Approval.
[p39]
[p39] It should preserve, where applicable:
[p39]
[p39]    ● exact Pricing Proposal version;
[p39]    ● applicable organisational policy;
[p39]    ● qualification conditions and evaluation result;
[p39]    ● submitting or invoking user and relevant authority context;
[p39]    ● relevant Guardrail configuration and evaluation;
[p39]    ● authorisation timestamp.
[p39]
[p39] Policy Authorisation must remain distinguishable from human Approval and must bind only
[p39] to the exact controlled Proposal version evaluated.
[p39]
[p39] A materially changed Proposal must complete a new policy-authorisation evaluation or other
[p39] applicable organisational decision path before receiving new execution authority.
[p39]
[p39]
[p39] 10.   Execution Record
[p39]
[p39]
[p39] An Execution Record captures what happened after a Pricing Proposal received valid
[p39] execution authority through approval or policy authorisation.
[p39]
[p39] It should establish:
[p39]
[p39]    ● what pricing action received execution authority;
[p39]
[p40]    ● what was actually implemented;
[p40]    ● who implemented or recorded it;
[p40]    ● when implementation occurred;
[p40]    ● implementation status;
[p40]    ● any implementation notes or exceptions.
[p40]
[p40] The MVP therefore preserves continuity between decision and execution, even where the
[p40] actual operational price update occurs outside Owómidé.
[p40]
[p40]
[p40] 11.   Evidence and Collaboration
[p40]
[p40]
[p40] Pricing work should support contextual collaboration directly around the relevant initiative,
[p40] proposal, rule, scenario or other pricing object.
[p40]
[p40] This can include:
[p40]
[p40]    ● comments;
[p40]    ● supporting documents;
[p40]    ● links;
[p40]    ● manually entered observations;
[p40]    ● stakeholder input;
[p40]    ● decisions;
[p40]    ● mentions and assignments.
[p40]
[p40] Collaboration should remain attached to the pricing work it relates to rather than becoming a
[p40] disconnected general conversation.
[p40]
[p40]
[p40] 12.   Activity and Audit History
[p40]
[p40]
[p40] Every consequential action should contribute to a persistent chronological history.
[p40]
[p40] The history should capture relevant events such as:
[p40]
[p40]    ● creation;
[p40]    ● edits;
[p40]    ● version changes;
[p40]    ● comments;
[p40]    ● assignments;
[p40]    ● submissions;
[p40]    ● reviews;
[p40]
[p41]     ● Approval and Policy Authorisation events;
[p41]     ● rejections;
[p41]     ● guardrail events;
[p41]     ● execution;
[p41]     ● status changes.
[p41]
[p41] This provides both operational visibility and historical accountability.
[p41]
[p41]
[p41] 13.    AI Participation
[p41]
[p41]
[p41] AI operates across these product objects rather than existing as an isolated system.
[p41]
[p41] AI may assist with a Pricing Initiative, strategy, analysis, scenario, proposal, collaboration
[p41] thread or historical context.
[p41]
[p41] AI-generated work must:
[p41]
[p41]     ● remain attributable as AI-assisted;
[p41]     ● operate within the invoking user's permissions;
[p41]     ● use only information the user is authorised to access;
[p41]     ● remain reviewable by human users;
[p41]     ● require explicit authorised-user action before AI-generated content is applied to
[p41]         consequential pricing work.
[p41]
[p41] AI does not independently approve or authorise consequential pricing actions.
[p41]
[p41]
[p41] Core Product Relationship
[p41]
[p41] The core MVP relationship is:
[p41]
[p41] Organisation Workspace
[p41] → Pricing Initiative
[p41] → Pricing Scope + Context + Strategy/Logic + Analysis/Scenarios
[p41] → Pricing Proposal
[p41] → Workflow and Collaboration
[p41] → Organisational Decision Authority
[p41] → Execution
[p41] → Monitoring and Historical Record
[p41]
[p41] The Pricing Initiative is the connective object that holds this lifecycle together.
[p41]
[p42] Organisational Decision Authority may result from item-level Approval or valid Policy
[p42] Authorisation according to the applicable controlled decision path.

[p43] 2.2 Product Lifecycle
[p43]
[p44]                                     PRODUCT LIFECYCLE
[p44]
[p44]
[p44] Pricing Initiative Lifecycle
[p44]
[p44] A Pricing Initiative should represent the complete lifecycle of a piece of pricing work from
[p44] initiation through execution, monitoring and closure.
[p44]
[p44] The lifecycle must track the business state of the pricing work, not merely the screen a
[p44] user is currently viewing.
[p44]
[p44]
[p44]  1.   Initiated
[p44]
[p44]
[p44] A Pricing Initiative begins when a pricing need, opportunity, request or trigger is formally
[p44] created in Owómidé.
[p44]
[p44] At minimum, the initiative should establish:
[p44]
[p44]    ● title;
[p44]    ● pricing objective or reason;
[p44]    ● initiative type;
[p44]    ● owner;
[p44]    ● pricing scope;
[p44]    ● urgency;
[p44]    ● relevant collaborators;
[p44]    ● initial supporting context.
[p44]
[p44] The initiative then moves into active development.
[p44]
[p44]
[p44]  2.   In Development
[p44]
[p44]
[p44] This is the main working state.
[p44]
[p44] Users can:
[p44]
[p44]    ● gather and attach relevant context;
[p44]    ● develop pricing strategy and logic;
[p44]    ● define assumptions and parameters;
[p44]    ● perform calculations and analysis;
[p44]    ● create scenarios;
[p44]
[p45]    ● assess unit economics;
[p45]    ● collaborate with stakeholders;
[p45]    ● use AI assistance;
[p45]    ● construct the proposed pricing action.
[p45]
[p45] An initiative may remain in this state through multiple iterations.
[p45]
[p45]
[p45]  3.   In Review
[p45]
[p45]
[p45] When the pricing work is sufficiently developed, it can move into review.
[p45]
[p45] Relevant stakeholders can:
[p45]
[p45]    ● inspect the pricing context;
[p45]    ● review assumptions and analysis;
[p45]    ● comment;
[p45]    ● request clarification;
[p45]    ● identify issues;
[p45]    ● recommend changes;
[p45]    ● confirm readiness for formal decision-making.
[p45]
[p45] Review is distinct from the organisational decision that may subsequently establish
[p45] Approved or Authorised Within Policy status.
[p45]
[p45] If material changes are required, the initiative returns to In Development.
[p45]
[p45]
[p45]  4.   Awaiting Approval
[p45]
[p45]
[p45] Once applicable review and Proposal-readiness requirements are satisfied, the Pricing
[p45] Initiative enters Awaiting Approval while the current Pricing Proposal progresses through the
[p45] applicable organisational decision path.
[p45]
[p45] The organisational decision path may be:
[p45]
[p45]    ● item-level human approval; or
[p45]    ● configured policy authorisation where that capability is enabled and the Proposal
[p45]        qualifies.
[p45]
[p45] At entry into the organisational decision path:
[p45]
[p46]    ● the current Draft Pricing Proposal version is frozen as the controlled decision
[p46]        snapshot;
[p46]    ● the exact affected Pricing Scope is resolved;
[p46]    ● the applicable organisational decision path is identified;
[p46]    ● relevant Workflow and Governance requirements are established;
[p46]    ● applicable Guardrail configuration and authoritative Guardrail evaluation are
[p46]        preserved as part of the decision context;
[p46]    ● the submitting user and submission timestamp are preserved;
[p46]    ● subsequent material changes require a new controlled Proposal version.
[p46]
[p46] Where item-level approval applies, the controlled Proposal version progresses from Draft to
[p46] Awaiting Approval and the required approvers and approval sequence are determined
[p46] through the applicable Workflow and Governance rules.
[p46]
[p46] Where configured policy authorisation applies, the same controlled Proposal version is
[p46] evaluated against the applicable policy-authorisation requirements.
[p46]
[p46] A Proposal validly qualifying for policy authorisation may progress from Draft to Authorised
[p46] Within Policy without being represented as having received human Approval.
[p46]
[p46] The applicable Workflow may vary through the supported Workflow Configuration Profile, but
[p46] it must remain within the canonical Pricing Initiative lifecycle and bounded decision models
[p46] defined by this PRD.
[p46]
[p46]
[p46]  5.   Decision Gate
[p46]
[p46]
[p46] The applicable organisational decision path can produce four relevant outcomes.
[p46]
[p46] Approved
[p46] The required authority has approved the submitted proposal version.
[p46]
[p46] Changes Requested
[p46] The proposal requires modification and returns to development or review. Any revised
[p46] submission becomes a new version.
[p46]
[p46] Rejected
[p46] The submitted proposal is rejected. The initiative may be revised and resubmitted or closed
[p46] without execution.
[p46]
[p47] Authorised Within Policy
[p47] Where the organisation has explicitly enabled predefined within-guardrail autonomy,
[p47] qualifying pricing work may proceed without item-level approval. The system records the
[p47] action as authorised under the applicable policy, not as an approval.
[p47]
[p47] No user may approve their own submitted pricing proposal.
[p47]
[p47]
[p47]  6.   Ready for Execution
[p47]
[p47]
[p47] Once a proposal has received all required approvals, or has been validly authorised within
[p47] policy, it becomes eligible for execution.
[p47]
[p47] At this stage the initiative should clearly show:
[p47]
[p47]    ● authorised pricing action;
[p47]    ● exact pricing proposal version providing execution authority;
[p47]    ● effective date;
[p47]    ● implementation owner;
[p47]    ● execution instructions;
[p47]    ● affected pricing scope;
[p47]    ● any conditions attached to the decision.
[p47]
[p47] An unapproved or unauthorised pricing action cannot be marked as implemented.
[p47]
[p47]
[p47]  7.   Implemented
[p47]
[p47]
[p47] The implementation owner records that the authorised pricing action has been implemented.
[p47]
[p47] The execution record should capture:
[p47]
[p47]    ● what was implemented;
[p47]    ● implementation date and time;
[p47]    ● implementing user;
[p47]    ● actual implemented price or pricing state;
[p47]    ● implementation notes;
[p47]    ● deviations from the authorised decision baseline, if any.
[p47]
[p47] Where the implemented state differs materially from the authorised decision baseline, the
[p47] discrepancy must remain visible rather than silently replacing or reinterpreting the authorised
[p47] Pricing Proposal version.
[p47]
[p48]  8.   Monitoring
[p48]
[p48]
[p48] After implementation, the initiative can enter a monitoring state.
[p48]
[p48] Users should be able to record or assess relevant outcomes such as:
[p48]
[p48]    ● sales or transaction movement;
[p48]    ● margin impact;
[p48]    ● revenue impact;
[p48]    ● volume changes;
[p48]    ● commercial feedback;
[p48]    ● customer response;
[p48]    ● observed issues;
[p48]    ● whether the pricing objective was achieved.
[p48]
[p48] Monitoring requirements may differ by initiative type and organisation.
[p48]
[p48]
[p48]  9.   Completed
[p48]
[p48]
[p48] A Pricing Initiative becomes completed when:
[p48]
[p48]    ● the required pricing work has finished;
[p48]    ● the final decision is known;
[p48]    ● execution status is resolved;
[p48]    ● required monitoring or outcome information has been recorded where applicable.
[p48]
[p48] Completion makes the initiative part of the organisation's permanent pricing history.
[p48]
[p48] Completed initiatives remain searchable and available as context for future pricing work.
[p48]
[p48]
[p48] 10.   Supporting Lifecycle States
[p48]
[p48]
[p48] The MVP should also support:
[p48]
[p48] On Hold
[p48] Work is temporarily paused without being cancelled. On Hold is a temporary supporting state
[p48] and does not change the underlying lifecycle stage from which the initiative was paused.
[p48]
[p49] Cancelled
[p49] The Pricing Initiative is intentionally terminated before completion, with the reason recorded.
[p49]
[p49] Expiration applies to Pricing Proposal Version Validity and, where relevant, to whether
[p49] previously established decision authority remains current for progression or execution.
[p49] Expiration does not apply to the Pricing Initiative itself and must not erase the historical
[p49] Approval or Policy Authorisation record.
[p49]
[p49] These conditions must preserve all previous work and activity history.
[p49]
[p49]
[p49] Revision and Versioning Behaviour
[p49]
[p49] Pricing work is expected to move backward as well as forward.
[p49]
[p49] The lifecycle must therefore support controlled loops such as:
[p49]
[p49] Pricing Proposal Changes Requested → Pricing Initiative returns to In Development or In
[p49] Review → revised Pricing Proposal version is submitted into the applicable organisational
[p49] decision path
[p49]
[p49] A material change after a Pricing Proposal has received Approval or Policy Authorisation
[p49] must not inherit the execution authority attached to the earlier Proposal version.
[p49]
[p49] The materially changed pricing work must create a new controlled Proposal version and
[p49] complete the applicable organisational decision requirements before receiving execution
[p49] authority.
[p49]
[p49] Previous Approval or Policy Authorisation records must remain historically attached to the
[p49] exact Proposal version against which they were made.
[p49]
[p49] Previous Pricing Proposal versions, organisational decisions, Approval records and Policy
[p49] Authorisation records must never be overwritten.
[p49]
[p49]
[p49] Urgent Pricing Work
[p49]
[p49] Urgency changes the speed and priority of the lifecycle, not the integrity of the lifecycle.
[p49]
[p49] Urgent initiatives can receive:
[p49]
[p49]     ● priority routing;
[p49]     ● accelerated notifications;
[p49]
[p50]     ● shorter response windows;
[p50]     ● escalation timers;
[p50]     ● alternate authorised approvers where configured.
[p50]
[p50] Urgency must not automatically remove required governance controls.
[p50]
[p50]
[p50]
[p50]
[p50] Canonical Lifecycle
[p50]
[p50] The canonical Pricing Initiative lifecycle is:
[p50]
[p50] Initiated → In Development → In Review → Awaiting Approval → Ready for Execution
[p50] → Implemented → Monitoring → Completed
[p50]
[p50] Approval and Policy Authorisation are organisational decision outcomes applied to the
[p50] relevant Pricing Proposal rather than separate Pricing Initiative lifecycle states.
[p50]
[p50] While the Pricing Initiative is Awaiting Approval, the applicable Pricing Proposal may
[p50] become:
[p50]
[p50]     ● Approved;
[p50]     ● Authorised Within Policy;
[p50]     ● Changes Requested;
[p50]     ● Rejected.
[p50]
[p50] Where the Pricing Proposal becomes Approved or validly Authorised Within Policy, and all
[p50] applicable execution conditions are satisfied, the Pricing Initiative may progress to Ready for
[p50] Execution.
[p50]
[p50] Where changes are requested, the Pricing Initiative returns to the appropriate In
[p50] Development or In Review state.
[p50]
[p50] Where a proposal is rejected, the Pricing Initiative may return for further development,
[p50] remain open for additional analysis, or be cancelled.
[p50]
[p51] 2.3 Capability Architecture
[p51]
[p52]                                 CAPABILITY ARCHITECTURE
[p52]
[p52] The MVP consists of interconnected capability systems that together allow pricing work to
[p52] move through Owómidé from initiation to completion.
[p52]
[p52] The capability architecture below maps directly to the functional areas defined in Sections
[p52] 3.1 through 3.14.
[p52]
[p52] Each capability has a primary functional owner in Section 3.0.
[p52]
[p52] Capabilities may consume records, rules, evaluations or outputs owned by another
[p52] functional area where required by the pricing lifecycle, but doing so must not create a
[p52] second canonical owner or independently redefine the consumed product contract.
[p52]
[p52]
[p52]    1. Organisation Workspace and Access Management
[p52]
[p52] Provides the shared environment in which an organisation's pricing work takes place.
[p52]
[p52] It must support:
[p52]
[p52]    ● organisation setup;
[p52]    ● users and teams;
[p52]    ● workspace configuration;
[p52]    ● access to active and historical pricing work;
[p52]    ● organisation-level pricing settings and policies.
[p52]
[p52]    2. Product and Pricing Context Management
[p52]
[p52] Provides the structured business and pricing context required to perform consequential
[p52] pricing work.
[p52]
[p52] It must support:
[p52]
[p52]    ● Priceable Entities, including supported product, SKU or variant, service and other
[p52]        directly priceable offering types;
[p52]    ● categories, product groups and other organisational groupings used to organise or
[p52]        select Priceable Entities;
[p52]    ● pricing dimensions such as location, region, channel, customer group, business unit,
[p52]        vendor and price list;
[p52]    ● Price Records and Current Price;
[p52]
[p53]    ● Cost Records and Applicable Unit Cost context;
[p53]    ● manual data entry;
[p53]    ● structured CSV import;
[p53]    ● bounded external pricing-context ingestion where supported;
[p53]    ● validation, provenance and freshness of pricing-context information;
[p53]    ● preservation of decision-relevant historical pricing context.
[p53]
[p53] Categories, product groups and pricing dimensions must remain distinguishable from the
[p53] underlying Priceable Entities.
[p53]
[p53] Current Price must follow the Price Record model defined in Section 3.2 rather than
[p53] represent an independent pricing object.
[p53]
[p53] Cost-dependent consequential calculations must use the Applicable Unit Cost defined in
[p53] Section 3.2 rather than an arbitrary Cost Record.
[p53]
[p53] The MVP must provide sufficient business context to perform real pricing work without
[p53] requiring customers to rebuild their ERP, accounting system or complete operational data
[p53] environment inside Owómidé.
[p53]
[p53]
[p53]    3. Pricing Initiative Management
[p53]
[p53] Provides the container through which pricing work is created and managed.
[p53]
[p53] It must support:
[p53]
[p53]    ● creating initiatives;
[p53]    ● defining objectives;
[p53]    ● selecting pricing scope;
[p53]    ● assigning ownership and collaborators;
[p53]    ● tracking initiative status;
[p53]    ● managing urgency and timelines;
[p53]    ● maintaining all related pricing work within one initiative.
[p53]
[p53]    4. Collaborative AI Pricing Builder
[p53]
[p53] The Pricing Builder is the core working surface in which users construct the pricing approach
[p53] associated with a Pricing Initiative.
[p53]
[p53] It must support structured construction of:
[p53]
[p54]     ● pricing actions;
[p54]     ● pricing strategies and rules;
[p54]     ● parameters;
[p54]     ● assumptions;
[p54]     ● initiative-specific conditions;
[p54]     ● initiative-specific constraints;
[p54]     ● thresholds contained within the pricing logic;
[p54]     ● affected Pricing Scope;
[p54]     ● entity-level exceptions where applicable;
[p54]     ● supported deterministic pricing outputs.
[p54]
[p54] The Builder must be able to reference applicable organisational Guardrails but must not
[p54] independently create, redefine or own those Guardrails.
[p54]
[p54] Organisational Guardrail definition and configuration remain governed by the Governance
[p54] capability.
[p54]
[p54] Deterministic calculations and Guardrail evaluations used by the Builder must consume the
[p54] authoritative outputs of the Pricing Analysis and Scenario Evaluation capability.
[p54]
[p54] The Builder should support both straightforward price changes and more structured pricing
[p54] approaches without requiring users to write code.
[p54]
[p54]
[p54]     5. Pricing Analysis and Scenario Evaluation
[p54]
[p54] Provides the deterministic analytical environment required to understand the financial and
[p54] commercial implications of pricing work before formal organisational decision.
[p54]
[p54] It must support, where applicable:
[p54]
[p54]     ● Current Price or another explicitly identified pricing baseline;
[p54]     ● proposed selling price;
[p54]     ● absolute and percentage price change where validly calculable;
[p54]     ● Applicable Unit Cost and relevant cost basis;
[p54]     ● gross profit;
[p54]     ● gross margin;
[p54]     ● markup;
[p54]     ● deterministic target-margin and target-markup calculations;
[p54]     ● supported promotional, discount and markdown calculations;
[p54]
[p55]    ● alternative pricing Scenarios;
[p55]    ● Scenario comparison;
[p55]    ● deterministic evaluation of applicable organisational Guardrails;
[p55]    ● calculation traceability;
[p55]    ● preservation of decision-relevant analytical context.
[p55]
[p55] Alternative analytical baselines must remain distinguishable from Current Price.
[p55]
[p55] Guardrail evaluation must consume Governance-defined Guardrails and must not
[p55] independently create or redefine organisational Guardrails.
[p55]
[p55] The MVP analytical capability is intended to support structured pricing decisions and
[p55] Scenario evaluation rather than advanced autonomous pricing optimisation.
[p55]
[p55]
[p55]    6. Pricing Proposal Management
[p55]
[p55] Provides the formal decision object through which sufficiently developed pricing work is
[p55] packaged for organisational decision.
[p55]
[p55] A Pricing Proposal represents the specific pricing action being submitted for decision and
[p55] remains distinct from the broader Pricing Initiative, exploratory Scenarios and editable
[p55] Builder work.
[p55]
[p55] It must support:
[p55]
[p55]    ● creation of a Pricing Proposal from sufficiently developed Initiative work;
[p55]    ● identification of the exact proposed pricing action and affected Pricing Scope;
[p55]    ● Proposal identifiers and controlled versions;
[p55]    ● Proposal readiness;
[p55]    ● a controlled decision snapshot at formal submission;
[p55]    ● Proposal status;
[p55]    ● Proposal version validity;
[p55]    ● distinction between Proposal status and approval progress;
[p55]    ● material-change handling;
[p55]    ● supersession and expiration;
[p55]    ● preservation of historical Proposal versions;
[p55]    ● binding organisational decision and execution authority to the exact controlled
[p55]        Proposal version.
[p55]
[p56] Pricing Proposal Management must not independently perform approval routing, grant
[p56] approval authority, grant policy authorisation or execute the pricing action.
[p56]
[p56] Those responsibilities remain with Workflow, Governance and Execution.
[p56]
[p56]
[p56]    7. Collaboration and Evidence Management
[p56]
[p56] Allows relevant stakeholders to contribute around the same pricing work while preserving the
[p56] context to which each contribution relates.
[p56]
[p56] It must support:
[p56]
[p56]    ● contextual comments;
[p56]    ● mentions;
[p56]    ● assignments;
[p56]    ● supporting evidence and attachments;
[p56]    ● manual market observations where relevant;
[p56]    ● stakeholder contributions;
[p56]    ● review feedback;
[p56]    ● resolution of relevant collaboration items;
[p56]    ● visibility into consequential activity and changes;
[p56]    ● association of collaboration and evidence with the relevant Initiative, Proposal,
[p56]        version or other pricing object.
[p56]
[p56] Collaboration must remain attached to the pricing work it concerns rather than becoming a
[p56] separate general-purpose messaging system.
[p56]
[p56] Evidence and collaboration must not independently become authoritative pricing data,
[p56] approval authority or execution authority merely because they are attached to pricing work.
[p56]
[p56]
[p56]    8. Workflow, Review and Approval Management
[p56]
[p56] Controls how Pricing Initiatives and Pricing Proposals progress through required review,
[p56] organisational decision and execution-readiness processes.
[p56]
[p56] It must support:
[p56]
[p56]    ● pricing-specific workflow assignment;
[p56]    ● required review requirements within the canonical In Review stage;
[p56]    ● review completion requirements;
[p56]
[p57]    ● item-level approval where applicable;
[p57]    ● configured policy-authorisation routing where applicable;
[p57]    ● approval routing;
[p57]    ● supported sequential or multiple-approval requirements;
[p57]    ● maker-checker enforcement;
[p57]    ● revision loops;
[p57]    ● Changes Requested treatment;
[p57]    ● rejection treatment;
[p57]    ● bounded urgency, deadline and escalation handling;
[p57]    ● supported approval delegation where organisational policy permits;
[p57]    ● execution-readiness gating;
[p57]    ● workflow visibility and attributable workflow history.
[p57]
[p57] The workflow capability must operate against the exact controlled Pricing Proposal version
[p57] and must not independently redefine Proposal content, Governance rules, Guardrail
[p57] calculations or decision snapshots.
[p57]
[p57] Workflow configuration must operate within the canonical Pricing Initiative lifecycle and the
[p57] bounded configuration model defined in Section 2.4.
[p57]
[p57] The MVP must not allow Workflow configuration to create arbitrary lifecycle states, free-form
[p57] workflow graphs, user-authored scripts, custom executable expressions, arbitrary triggers or
[p57] general-purpose automation.
[p57]
[p57] Organisational workflow differences must be represented through supported pricing-specific
[p57] configuration parameters.
[p57]
[p57]
[p57]    9. Roles, Permissions and Pricing Governance
[p57]
[p57] Controls who may view, create, modify, review, approve, authorise, execute or administer
[p57] consequential pricing work.
[p57]
[p57] It must support:
[p57]
[p57]    ● role-based permissions;
[p57]    ● organisational and pricing-scope authority;
[p57]    ● sensitive commercial and cost-data access;
[p57]    ● separation of duties where required;
[p57]    ● approval authority and limits;
[p57]
[p58]    ● organisational Guardrail definition and configuration;
[p58]    ● Guardrail behavioural treatment;
[p58]    ● policy-authorisation configuration;
[p58]    ● governance enforcement;
[p58]    ● prevention of unauthorised execution;
[p58]    ● attributable governance changes;
[p58]    ● historical governance context.
[p58]
[p58] Governance must be enforced by the system rather than depend solely on documented
[p58] organisational procedures.
[p58]
[p58] Governance defines applicable organisational controls but must not independently perform
[p58] deterministic pricing calculations or Guardrail calculations owned by the analytical capability.
[p58]
[p58]
[p58]    10.    AI Assistance and AI Governance
[p58]
[p58]
[p58] AI operates across the workspace as a contextual assistant to authorised users.
[p58]
[p58] AI may assist with activities such as:
[p58]
[p58]    ● understanding pricing context;
[p58]    ● drafting or refining pricing work;
[p58]    ● translating business intent into structured pricing logic;
[p58]    ● interpreting pricing analysis;
[p58]    ● comparing Scenarios;
[p58]    ● identifying missing or inconsistent information;
[p58]    ● explaining deterministic calculations;
[p58]    ● explaining Guardrail results;
[p58]    ● summarising stakeholder input;
[p58]    ● retrieving relevant historical pricing context;
[p58]    ● preparing pricing work for review;
[p58]    ● summarising recorded post-implementation information.
[p58]
[p58] AI must operate within the invoking user's access and authority.
[p58]
[p58] AI must not independently:
[p58]
[p58]    ● approve or policy-authorise a Pricing Proposal;
[p58]    ● provide execution authority;
[p58]    ● execute a pricing action;
[p58]
[p59]    ● become the authoritative deterministic calculation engine;
[p59]    ● become the authoritative Guardrail-evaluation engine;
[p59]    ● bypass organisational permissions or Governance;
[p59]    ● silently apply consequential changes to pricing work.
[p59]
[p59] AI-generated or AI-assisted changes that are applied to pricing work must remain
[p59] attributable according to the applicable functional requirements.
[p59]
[p59]
[p59]    11.    Execution and Post-Implementation Monitoring
[p59]
[p59]
[p59] Provides continuity between organisational pricing decision authority and the actual
[p59] operational outcome.
[p59]
[p59] It must support:
[p59]
[p59]    ● identification of the exact Approved or Authorised Within Policy Pricing Proposal
[p59]        version providing execution authority;
[p59]    ● assignment of implementation responsibility;
[p59]    ● structured execution instructions;
[p59]    ● recording implementation;
[p59]    ● recording the actual implemented pricing state;
[p59]    ● comparison of authorised and implemented pricing;
[p59]    ● identification of execution variance;
[p59]    ● partial implementation and Implementation Exceptions;
[p59]    ● establishment or update of the applicable Current Price where implementation is
[p59]        confirmed and Owómidé maintains the relevant Price Record;
[p59]    ● recording relevant post-implementation results and observations;
[p59]    ● preservation of implementation history and outcome context.
[p59]
[p59] The capability must preserve the distinction between:
[p59]
[p59] Authorised Pricing Action → Actual Implementation → Observed Outcome
[p59]
[p59] Execution must not independently modify or reinterpret the controlled Pricing Proposal
[p59] version that provides its authority.
[p59]
[p59]
[p59]    12.    Versioning, Audit Trail and Pricing Knowledge
[p59]
[p59]
[p59] Provides persistent organisational memory around pricing.
[p59]
[p60] It must support:
[p60]
[p60]    ● version history;
[p60]    ● chronological activity history;
[p60]    ● attributable user actions;
[p60]    ● organisational decision history, including Approval and Policy Authorisation records;
[p60]    ● decision rationale;
[p60]    ● execution history;
[p60]    ● historical pricing initiatives;
[p60]    ● search and retrieval of previous pricing work.
[p60]
[p60] Historical records must not be silently overwritten.
[p60]
[p60]
[p60]    13.    Notifications, Work Queues and Visibility
[p60]
[p60]
[p60] Keeps participants aware of relevant pricing work requiring their attention without becoming
[p60] an independent source of workflow or decision state.
[p60]
[p60] It must support notifications for relevant events such as:
[p60]
[p60]    ● assignments;
[p60]    ● mentions;
[p60]    ● review requests;
[p60]    ● approval requests;
[p60]    ● requested changes;
[p60]    ● approvals;
[p60]    ● policy authorisations;
[p60]    ● rejections;
[p60]    ● escalations;
[p60]    ● approaching deadlines;
[p60]    ● execution assignments;
[p60]    ● execution-readiness events.
[p60]
[p60] Users should also have role-appropriate work queues and workspace-level visibility into:
[p60]
[p60]    ● work requiring their attention;
[p60]    ● active Pricing Initiatives;
[p60]    ● review work;
[p60]    ● pending approvals;
[p60]
[p61]    ● required actions;
[p61]    ● execution responsibilities;
[p61]    ● recently completed work;
[p61]    ● relevant pricing activity.
[p61]
[p61] Notifications, work queues and visibility surfaces must project authoritative underlying
[p61] product state rather than independently create or redefine that state.
[p61]
[p61]
[p61]
[p61]
[p61]    14.    Data Export and External Operational Handoff
[p61]
[p61]
[p61] Provides practical mechanisms for taking supported pricing information and authorised
[p61] pricing actions from Owómidé into the organisation's external operating environment.
[p61]
[p61] It must support, where applicable:
[p61]
[p61]    ● structured export of pricing information;
[p61]    ● export of exact authorised Pricing Proposal versions for operational implementation;
[p61]    ● external identifiers required for supported handoff;
[p61]    ● human-readable operational handoff;
[p61]    ● export validation;
[p61]    ● export attribution and auditability;
[p61]    ● bounded integration with selected external systems;
[p61]    ● distinction between export or handoff and confirmed implementation;
[p61]    ● relationship between authorised action, handed-off action and actual implementation.
[p61]
[p61] A supported connector may also exchange inbound pricing-context information where the
[p61] connector allows it.
[p61]
[p61] Where external information enters Owómidé as pricing context, the semantic ownership of
[p61] that information remains with Product and Pricing Context Management.
[p61]
[p61] Externally received selling-price information must therefore enter the Price Record model,
[p61] and externally received cost information must enter the Cost Record model, before being
[p61] used as authoritative Owómidé pricing context.
[p61]
[p61] The availability of specific native connectors must be driven by validated customer or pilot
[p61] requirements.
[p61]
[p62] The MVP must not require broad connector coverage, universal bidirectional synchronisation
[p62] or autonomous publishing into external systems.
[p62]
[p62]
[p62] Capability Relationship
[p62]
[p62] The core pricing-work flow is:
[p62]
[p62] Product and Pricing Context → Pricing Initiative → Collaborative AI Pricing Builder →
[p62] Analysis and Scenarios → Collaboration → Pricing Proposal → Review and Organisational
[p62] Decision Workflow → Execution → Monitoring → Audit and Pricing Knowledge
[p62]
[p62] The Pricing Proposal is the formal decision object between developed pricing work and
[p62] organisational decision authority.
[p62]
[p62] Organisation Workspace and Access Management, Roles, Permissions and Pricing
[p62] Governance, AI Assistance and AI Governance, Notifications, Work Queues and Visibility,
[p62] and Versioning, Audit Trail and Pricing Knowledge operate across the lifecycle rather than as
[p62] isolated sequential stages.
[p62]
[p62] Governance defines applicable organisational controls.
[p62]
[p62] The analytical capability performs authoritative deterministic calculations and Guardrail
[p62] evaluations.
[p62]
[p62] Workflow consumes the controlled Proposal and applicable Governance to establish the
[p62] required organisational decision path.
[p62]
[p62] Execution consumes the exact Proposal version that received valid execution authority.
[p62]
[p62] Data Export and External Operational Handoff bridges authorised Owómidé outputs into
[p62] supported external operating processes.
[p62]
[p62] Inbound pricing context received through a supported connector must enter the canonical
[p62] Product and Pricing Context models before being treated as Owómidé pricing context.
[p62]
[p62] Historical records must preserve the exact Proposal, Governance, workflow, execution and
[p62] pricing context relevant to the decision at the time.
[p62]
[p63] 2.4 Scope Boundary
[p63]
[p64]                                      SCOPE BOUNDARY
[p64]
[p64] The MVP must deliver a complete, usable pricing-workflow experience rather than a broad
[p64] collection of partially implemented pricing capabilities.
[p64]
[p64] A capability belongs in the MVP only when a business must perform real pricing work from
[p64] initiation through analysis, collaboration, organisational decision, execution recording and
[p64] monitoring inside Owómidé.
[p64]
[p64]
[p64]  1.   In Scope
[p64]
[p64]
[p64] The MVP includes:
[p64]
[p64]    ● organisation workspace setup and management;
[p64]    ● user, team, role and permission management;
[p64]    ● Priceable Entity management for supported products, SKUs or variants, services and
[p64]        other directly priceable offerings, plus category and product-group management;
[p64]    ● manual and CSV-based pricing data import;
[p64]    ● bounded external integrations with selected business, spreadsheet, productivity,
[p64]        commerce or operational systems where those integrations materially reduce
[p64]        pricing-workflow friction, with specific connectors prioritised according to validated
[p64]        customer and pilot requirements;
[p64]    ● Price Records and Current Price, Cost Records and Applicable Unit Cost context;
[p64]    ● Pricing Initiative creation and lifecycle management;
[p64]    ● pricing scope selection;
[p64]    ● collaborative AI-native Pricing Builder;
[p64]    ● pricing     objectives,   strategies,   rules,   assumptions   and    parameters,   plus
[p64]        Governance-defined organisational Guardrails;
[p64]    ● unit-economics calculations;
[p64]    ● pricing scenario creation and comparison;
[p64]    ● contextual AI assistance;
[p64]    ● comments, mentions, assignments and supporting evidence;
[p64]    ● review and revision behaviour within the canonical Pricing Initiative lifecycle;
[p64]    ● bounded pricing-specific organisational decision routing using supported condition
[p64]        types, Approval rules and Policy Authorisation rules where applicable;
[p64]    ● maker-checker controls, item-level Approval authority and Policy Authorisation
[p64]        governance;
[p64]
[p65]    ● proposal creation and versioning;
[p65]    ● Approval, Policy Authorisation, Rejection and Changes Requested decision
[p65]        treatment where applicable;
[p65]    ● implementation assignment and recording;
[p65]    ● post-implementation monitoring records;
[p65]    ● activity history and audit trail;
[p65]    ● searchable historical pricing work;
[p65]    ● workspace notifications and work queues;
[p65]    ● structured export of pricing decisions and authorised pricing actions.
[p65]
[p65]  2.   MVP Configuration Boundary
[p65]
[p65]
[p65] The MVP must support bounded, pricing-specific configuration without becoming a
[p65] user-programmable workflow, rules or automation platform.
[p65]
[p65] Organisations should be able to configure supported organisational settings including:
[p65]
[p65]    ● roles and permissions;
[p65]    ● approval authority and organisational scope;
[p65]    ● organisational Guardrails and supported pricing thresholds;
[p65]    ● relevant pricing dimensions;
[p65]    ● initiative types;
[p65]    ● required fields and participants where supported;
[p65]    ● a bounded Workflow Configuration Profile.
[p65]
[p65] A Workflow Configuration Profile may configure supported workflow parameters such as:
[p65]
[p65]    ● whether formal review requirements apply within the canonical In Review stage;
[p65]    ● required reviewer roles or participants;
[p65]    ● the applicable organisational decision path;
[p65]    ● required approver roles;
[p65]    ● supported sequential or multiple-approval arrangements;
[p65]    ● approval routing conditions selected from supported pricing-specific condition types;
[p65]    ● target response dates or approval deadlines;
[p65]    ● supported escalation behaviour;
[p65]    ● authorised approval delegation where supported.
[p65]
[p65] Workflow configuration must operate within the canonical Pricing Initiative lifecycle defined in
[p65] Section 2.2.
[p65]
[p66] Workflow configuration must not:
[p66]
[p66]    ● create additional Pricing Initiative lifecycle states;
[p66]    ● remove or redefine the meaning of canonical lifecycle states;
[p66]    ● create arbitrary lifecycle transitions outside supported progression and revision
[p66]        behaviour;
[p66]    ● redefine Pricing Proposal statuses or Proposal version-validity states;
[p66]    ● execute user-authored scripts or code;
[p66]    ● evaluate arbitrary executable formulas or expressions as workflow logic;
[p66]    ● create unrestricted user-authored triggers or action chains;
[p66]    ● create arbitrary external actions or automation;
[p66]    ● automate general organisational processes unrelated to pricing;
[p66]    ● bypass Proposal readiness, approval authority, maker-checker controls, Guardrails,
[p66]        policy-authorisation conditions or other Governance requirements.
[p66]
[p66] Different organisational review and decision structures must therefore be represented
[p66] through supported Workflow Configuration Profile parameters rather than through
[p66] unrestricted workflow programming.
[p66]
[p66]
[p66]  3.   AI Boundary
[p66]
[p66]
[p66] AI is embedded throughout the workspace but remains assistive.
[p66]
[p66] AI may:
[p66]
[p66]    ● analyse;
[p66]    ● draft;
[p66]    ● summarise;
[p66]    ● explain;
[p66]    ● compare;
[p66]    ● identify risks;
[p66]    ● retrieve context;
[p66]    ● suggest alternatives;
[p66]    ● assist with calculations and pricing construction.
[p66]
[p66] AI may not independently:
[p66]
[p66]    ● approve or policy-authorise a Pricing Proposal;
[p66]    ● override permissions, Guardrails or other Governance controls;
[p66]
[p67]    ● execute consequential pricing actions;
[p67]    ● silently change pricing work that has received Approval or Policy Authorisation, or
[p67]        bypass the applicable Proposal-version and organisational decision requirements.
[p67]  4.   Execution Boundary
[p67]
[p67]
[p67] The MVP must maintain continuity through execution, but execution does not require
[p67] Owómidé to automatically publish prices into every operational system.
[p67]
[p67] The MVP must be able to record:
[p67]
[p67] what was proposed → what received organisational decision authority → what was
[p67] implemented → who implemented it → when it happened → whether implementation
[p67] differed from the authorised decision baseline.
[p67]
[p67] Manual implementation outside Owómidé remains valid for the MVP.
[p67]
[p67]
[p67]  5.   Monitoring Boundary
[p67]
[p67]
[p67] The MVP supports post-implementation monitoring sufficient to close the pricing lifecycle.
[p67]
[p67] Users can record or import relevant outcomes such as:
[p67]
[p67]    ● sales;
[p67]    ● volume;
[p67]    ● margin;
[p67]    ● revenue;
[p67]    ● customer or commercial feedback;
[p67]    ● observed pricing outcomes.
[p67]
[p67] The MVP is not required to automatically optimise future prices from these outcomes.
[p67]
[p67]
[p67]  6.   Explicitly Out of Scope
[p67]
[p67]
[p67] The MVP does not include:
[p67]
[p67]    ● autonomous repricing;
[p67]    ● unattended pricing execution;
[p67]    ● automated continuous competitor-price collection;
[p67]    ● proprietary offline market-data collection infrastructure;
[p67]    ● advanced machine-learning price optimisation;
[p67]
[p68]    ● automated elasticity modelling;
[p68]    ● autonomous demand forecasting;
[p68]    ● broad, autonomous, or unattended real-time price publishing across ERP, POS,
[p68]        marketplace, and commerce systems;
[p68]    ● broad enterprise connector coverage;
[p68]    ● hardware-based pricing infrastructure;
[p68]    ● general-purpose workflow automation unrelated to pricing;
[p68]    ● arbitrary user-defined Pricing Initiative lifecycle stages or free-form workflow graphs;
[p68]    ● user-authored workflow scripts, custom executable expressions, arbitrary triggers or
[p68]        arbitrary action chains;
[p68]    ● unrestricted user-programmable automation;
[p68]    ● AI acting as an approver or autonomous commercial authority.
[p68]
[p68] These capabilities must not become hidden dependencies for completing the MVP's core
[p68] pricing workflow.
[p68]
[p68]
[p68]  7.   MVP Completeness Test
[p68]
[p68]
[p68] The MVP scope is complete when a target organisation can:
[p68]
[p68] bring real pricing data into Owómidé → initiate real pricing work → construct and analyse the
[p68] pricing approach → collaborate → review and revise → apply organisational controls →
[p68] complete the applicable organisational decision path → establish execution authority →
[p68] record implementation → monitor the result → retrieve the complete decision history
[p68]
[p68] without requiring any out-of-scope capability for that workflow to succeed.
[p68]

[p73] Conditional Language
[p73]
[p73] Where applicable / Where relevant
[p73]
[p73] The requirement applies only when the stated condition exists.
[p73]
[p73] Where that condition applies, any associated Must or Must Not requirement remains
[p73] mandatory.
[p73]
[p74] At minimum
[p74]
[p74] Every listed item is mandatory.
[p74]
[p74] Additional supported items may also be provided.
[p74]
[p74]
[p74] Examples
[p74]
[p74] Terms such as:
[p74]    ● such as
[p74]    ● for example
[p74]    ● may include
[p74]
[p74] introduce illustrative examples unless the surrounding requirement explicitly makes the listed
[p74] examples mandatory.
[p74]
[p74] Examples must not silently expand the MVP scope.
[p74]
[p74]
[p74] Canonical Requirement References
[p74]
[p74] Where one requirement references another canonical requirement, the referenced
[p74] requirement remains authoritative for the underlying product contract. A consuming
[p74] requirement must not redefine, weaken or create a competing version of that contract.
[p74]
[p74]
[p74] Interpretation Principle
[p74]
[p74] Requirement strength must be determined by the language of the requirement, not by:
[p74]    ● feature prominence;
[p74]    ● interface placement;
[p74]    ● section length;
[p74]    ● implementation convenience;
[p74]    ● frequency of mention elsewhere in the PRD.
[p74]
[p74] A possible product behaviour must not be interpreted as mandatory merely because it is
[p74] described in the PRD.
[p74]
[p74] Mandatory MVP functional acceptance must ultimately be traceable to:
[p74]
[p74]    ● an explicit Must or Must Not requirement; or
[p74]    ● another mandatory requirement that makes the behaviour necessary.
